<mode>
Product documentation and evidence-based market research.
Prepare for a production build; do not begin implementation yet.
</mode>

<role>
Act as the product research lead. Delegate bounded research work
to agents, then verify and synthesize their findings.
</role>

<goal>
Organize the existing product knowledge and determine whether a
specific group of people will pay for Promptor's actual workflow:
fast prompting and reprompting, reusable context, context isolation,
and deliberate interaction with models throughout long conversations.
</goal>

<context>
I initially imagined roughly 35 customers. I have since seen related
products advertising around 20,000 users. Treat those figures as
unverified leads, not evidence of paying customers.

I have used or tried Obsidian, VS Code, Zettr, Joblin, Todoist, and
templates. Their existence alone does not establish that they meet
this need efficiently.

My evidence includes Claude, Gemini, and GPT conversations and videos
showing workflow speed differences. I am also collecting outputs where
inputs assembled with my tool appear to restore response quality deep
into a conversation. Treat these as observations to examine, not
established causal effects.
</context>

<phase_1_product_context>
1. Review the project files, prototypes, feature plans, ideas, and
   reference materials. Understand the application flow and feel.
2. Locate and read the previous chat's handoff or compaction summary.
   Recover relevant source context where available; flag gaps.
3. Fetch and read the artifact pitch:
   https://claude.ai/artifact/G4CcgxGCUu8rdivC99fYbo
   If inaccessible, report that limitation and continue with available
   material. Do not reconstruct its claims from assumptions.
4. Extract the pitch's propositions into a claim list.
5. Consolidate the product information into a clear source of truth:
   intended users, problems, workflows, requirements, prototype
   coverage, design decisions, unresolved questions, and build scope.
6. Distinguish settled decisions, proposals, observed functionality,
   and untested claims. Preserve source links and original materials.
   Review the organization once more for omissions and contradictions.
</phase_1_product_context>

<phase_2_agent_research>
Give each agent the shared product brief and bounded questions:

- Market evidence: identify direct and adjacent products, including
  Chrome/Google extensions, Android apps, and relevant Wispr features.
  Verify product identities and their actual relationship to Promptor.
- Customer needs: investigate recurring unmet needs, friction,
  abandonment, cancellation, and switching reasons.
- Commercial evidence: investigate pricing, purchases, paying
  subscribers, conversion, retention, and revenue where available.
- Workflow comparison: assess specialized products and general tools
  against Promptor's intended workflow and the supplied demonstrations.

Require sources and uncertainty labels. Check important claims yourself,
resolve disagreements, and combine the findings into one assessment.
Test and expand the pitch's propositions; do not merely confirm them.
</phase_2_agent_research>

<research_questions>
- Who experiences this problem often enough to consider paying?
- What do existing customers value, request, or find missing?
- What documented shortcomings caused people to leave or cancel?
- Why do these gaps persist: technical limits, product priorities,
  economics, discoverability, or something else?
- How many customers demonstrably paid, and at what price?
- Which gap could support an initial niche of roughly 35 paying users?
</research_questions>

<evidence_rules>
- Use current sources. Record dates, links, and what each supports.
- Separate downloads, installs, registered users, active users,
  paying customers, and revenue. Never substitute one for another.
- Distinguish independently supported facts, vendor claims,
  individual reports, estimates, and unknowns.
- Pricing proves an offer exists; it does not prove purchases.
  Report unavailable payment or retention data plainly.
- Treat firsthand complaints as qualitative evidence. Do not infer
  churn from dissatisfaction alone or market prevalence from a few posts.
- Evaluate "just use Obsidian or VS Code" through demonstrated workflows:
  setup effort, steps, time, repeated use, scale, context isolation,
  context reuse, and output quality. Accept strong evidence either way.
- Examine my demonstrations critically. Separate interface speed gains
  from prompt-content differences, model variation, and context effects.
  Identify the smallest useful comparison where evidence is missing.
</evidence_rules>

<deliverables>
1. Organized product documentation with an index and unresolved decisions.
2. Pitch-claim assessment: supported, contradicted, or unresolved,
   with evidence and implications.
3. Compact competitor table covering workflow fit, price, adoption
   metric, payment evidence, customer complaints, and evidence quality.
4. A recommendation identifying the most plausible initial customer,
   valuable differentiation, willingness-to-pay evidence, and remaining
   assumptions to test before a production commitment.

Lead with findings. Keep the main report concise; place detailed
sources and research notes in supporting files.
</deliverables>
